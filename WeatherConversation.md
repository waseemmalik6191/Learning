
# Everyday English: Weather Conversations

*A practical speaking handbook for everyday conversations in Ireland.*

---

## How to Use This Handbook

Read each conversation aloud slowly. Repeat it a second time with a natural rhythm, then try saying your part without looking at the page.

You do not need to speak quickly or use complicated words. Clear, short sentences are excellent English.

**Practice tips:**
- Read each conversation aloud.
- Focus on pronunciation and natural pauses.
- Practise both sides of the conversation.
- Try answering without looking at the model answers.
- Use these expressions when chatting with colleagues and friends.

---

# 1. Starting a Weather Conversation

Starting a conversation about the weather is one of the easiest ways to make small talk with colleagues in Ireland.

### Useful Openers

- Morning! How are you doing?
- It's a lovely day, isn't it?
- The weather is a bit miserable today, isn't it?
- It looks like rain.
- Have you checked the forecast?
- Hopefully, the weather will improve later.

---

# 2. Sunny Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** It's lovely outside today, isn't it?

**You:** Yeah, it's beautiful! I'm glad to see some sunshine.

**Colleague:** Hopefully, it'll stay like this for a few days.

**You:** I hope so. I'm not a big fan of cold and rainy weather.

### Useful Phrases

- It's nice and sunny.
- The weather has finally improved.
- I hope it stays sunny.
- I might go for a walk.
- It's lovely to get some sunshine.

---

# 3. Cloudy or Overcast Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** It's quite cloudy today, isn't it?

**You:** Yeah, it looks like the sun might not come out today.

**Colleague:** I think it might rain later.

**You:** It wouldn't surprise me! The weather changes so quickly here.

### Useful Phrases

- It's quite cloudy today.
- The sky is overcast.
- It looks a bit gloomy outside.
- Hopefully, it'll brighten up later.
- It doesn't look very promising outside.

---

# 4. Rainy Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Friend:** Did you get caught in the rain?

**You:** Yes! I was walking home when it suddenly started raining.

**Friend:** Oh no! Did you have an umbrella?

**You:** Unfortunately, I didn't. I got completely soaked.

### Useful Phrases

- It looks like rain.
- It's raining heavily.
- It's just drizzling.
- I got caught in the rain.
- I got completely soaked.
- Don't forget to take an umbrella.
- It's absolutely pouring outside.

# 5. Windy Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** It's really windy outside today.

**You:** I know! The wind is so strong.

**Colleague:** It's not very pleasant, is it?

**You:** Not really. I'd rather have a sunny, calm day.

### Useful Phrases

- It's really windy today.
- It's quite breezy.
- The wind is getting stronger.
- It's too windy to go for a walk.
- The wind is picking up.
- Hopefully, it'll calm down later.

# 6. Cold and Chilly Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Friend:** It's freezing this morning, isn't it?

**You:** Absolutely! I could barely feel my hands outside.

**Friend:** You should wear a warmer jacket.

**You:** Definitely. I wasn't expecting it to be this cold.

### Useful Phrases

- It's quite chilly today.
- It's absolutely freezing.
- I need to wrap up warm.
- I should have worn a thicker jacket.
- I can't wait to get somewhere warm.
- It's much colder than I expected.

# 7. Stormy Weather and Weather Warnings

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** Did you see the weather warning?

**You:** Yes, I heard there might be strong winds and heavy rain.

**Colleague:** Hopefully, it won't be too bad.

**You:** I hope so. I'd rather stay indoors if the weather gets worse.

### Useful Phrases

- There's a storm warning.
- The wind is really picking up.
- It's pouring down.
- There's a chance of thunderstorms.
- It's probably best to stay indoors.
- Hopefully, the storm will pass soon.

# 8. Foggy or Misty Weather

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** It was really foggy on my way to work.

**You:** Was it? I hope you drove carefully.

**Colleague:** Yes, visibility was quite poor.

**You:** Fog can make driving difficult. Hopefully, it'll clear up soon.

### Useful Phrases

- It's quite foggy this morning.
- I can barely see anything.
- Visibility is poor.
- The fog is starting to clear.
- Drive carefully; it's difficult to see.

# 9. When the Weather Changes Suddenly

### Conversation Practice

**Friend:** It was raining a minute ago, and now the sun's out!

**You:** I know! The weather here is so unpredictable.

**Friend:** That's Ireland for you!

**You:** Exactly! You can experience four seasons in one day.

### Useful Phrases

- The weather keeps changing.
- It's sunny one minute and raining the next.
- You never know what to expect.
- The rain has finally stopped.
- The weather is so unpredictable.
- It can change within minutes.

# 10. Hoping for Sunshine

### Conversation Practice

**Flatmate:** What are you hoping for tomorrow?

**You:** I'm hoping for some sunshine so I can dry my clothes.

**Flatmate:** The forecast looks a bit better.

**You:** That's good news. I'd love a dry, sunny day.

### Useful Phrases

- I hope it'll be sunny tomorrow.
- I'm hoping for some sunshine.
- It would be nice to get a bit of sun.
- Hopefully, it'll be dry tomorrow.
- I could really do with some sunshine.
- Let's hope the weather improves.

# 11. Comparing Weather in Ireland and India

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** What's winter like in India?

**You:** It depends on the region, but in my part of India, winter is usually much milder than here.

**Colleague:** Does it get very cold?

**You:** Sometimes, but temperatures are often around 15 to 20 degrees in winter where I'm from. It's generally warmer than Ireland.

### Useful Phrases

- In my part of India, winters are usually mild.
- The weather varies from region to region.
- It's generally warmer than here.
- I'm still getting used to the Irish weather.
- I prefer warmer weather.
- I'm more comfortable when the temperature is around 25 to 30 degrees.

# 12. Talking About the Weather Forecast

### Conversation Practice

**Colleague:** Have you checked tomorrow's forecast?

**You:** Yes. It looks like there might be heavy rain and strong winds.

**Colleague:** Hopefully, it won't be too bad.

**You:** I hope so. I'll keep an eye on the forecast.

### Useful Phrases

- The forecast says it might rain.
- There's a chance of showers.
- They've issued a weather warning.
- I'll keep an eye on the forecast.
- It looks like it'll be dry tomorrow.
- The forecast isn't looking great.

# 13. Weather Vocabulary

Use this section to build your weather-related vocabulary.

### Temperature

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hot | High temperature |
| Warm | Comfortably high temperature |
| Mild | Neither very cold nor very hot |
| Cool | Slightly cold |
| Chilly | Uncomfortably or noticeably cold |
| Freezing | Extremely cold |

### Rain

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Drizzle | Very light rain |

| Downpour | Heavy rain |

### Sky

|---|---|

### Wind

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|

### Other Weather Words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|

# 14. Useful Expressions and What They Mean

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Brighten up | Become sunnier | Hopefully, it'll brighten up later. |
| Clear up | Clouds, fog or rain begin to disappear | The fog should clear up soon. |
| Warm up | Become warmer | It might warm up this afternoon. |
| Cool down | Become cooler | It usually cools down in the evening. |
| Pour down | Rain heavily | It started pouring down just as I left. |
| Die down | Become less strong | I hope the wind dies down later. |
| Wrap up warm | Wear warm clothes | Wrap up warm; it's freezing outside. |
| Get caught in the rain | Be outside when it starts raining unexpectedly | I got caught in the rain on my way home. |
| Get soaked | Become very wet | I forgot my umbrella and got soaked. |
| Keep an eye on the forecast | Check the forecast from time to time | I'll keep an eye on the forecast. |

# 15. Make Your English Sound More Natural

Here are some common mistakes and their natural alternatives.

| Instead of Saying | Say This |
|---|---|
| Today weather is very cold. | It's really cold today. |
| I want sun. | I'm hoping for some sunshine. |
| Tomorrow will be sunny, I hope. | Hopefully, it'll be sunny tomorrow. |
| Rain is coming. | It looks like rain. |
| I became wet in rain. | I got caught in the rain. / I got soaked. |
| Weather is changing very fast. | The weather keeps changing. |
| Repeating "you know" | Pause briefly, then continue your sentence. |

### Speaking Tip

You don't need to fill every silence with words.

Instead of saying:

"Yeah, you know, the weather is, you know, really bad today."

Try:

"Yeah, the weather is really bad today."

A short pause sounds more natural than repeatedly using filler words.

# 16. Daily Speaking Practice

Practise answering these questions aloud. Aim for two or three sentences each time.

1. What's the weather like today?
2. Do you prefer sunny or rainy weather? Why?
3. Did you get caught in the rain recently?
4. What do you usually do when it's cold and windy?
5. What's winter like in your part of India?
6. Have you checked the forecast for tomorrow?
7. What kind of weather do you enjoy most?
8. Do you think Irish weather is unpredictable?
9. What's the coldest weather you've experienced in Ireland?
10. What do you usually wear when it's freezing outside?

### Challenge

Try answering each question without reading the examples.

Focus on:
- Giving a direct answer.
- Explaining your reason.
- Adding one personal example.

# 17. Final Practice Conversation

### Scenario: Chatting with a Colleague in the Morning

**Colleague:** Morning! It's absolutely pouring outside. Did you get caught in the rain on your way here?

**You:** Morning! Yes, I did. I didn't bring an umbrella, so I got a bit wet. Hopefully, the weather will improve later!

**Colleague:** I checked the forecast, and it might be windy all afternoon.

**You:** Oh, really? I'm hoping for some sunshine tomorrow. I need to dry my clothes.

**Colleague:** Hopefully, we'll get a bit of sunshine.

**You:** Fingers crossed! I'm not a big fan of cold and windy weather.

## Final Reminder

You do not need perfect English to have a good conversation.

- Speak clearly.
- Use short sentences.
- Pause when you need time to think.
- Ask questions to keep the conversation going.
- Don't worry too much about making mistakes.

**Remember: Clear communication is more important than complicated vocabulary. Practise a little every day, and your confidence will gradually improve.**
