# Part 1: Food and Farming in Ireland and India

## 1. Reading Passage: Food Habits in Ireland and India

### Why Are Food Habits Different in Ireland and India?

I've noticed some interesting differences between food habits in Ireland and India.

Whenever I visit supermarkets such as Lidl or Aldi, I often see potatoes, carrots, onions and cabbage. These vegetables are easy to find, and some of them are quite affordable.

In India, however, we have a wide variety of vegetables, herbs and spices. We use ingredients such as coriander, mint, curry leaves, green beans, okra and different varieties of brinjal. We also use spices such as cumin, turmeric, coriander seeds, ginger and garlic to prepare our everyday meals.

I think one of the main reasons for these differences is the climate. Ireland has a cooler and wetter climate, whereas many parts of India have warmer weather. Different crops grow better under different conditions.

Another reason could be people's food habits and local demand. Irish traditional cooking often includes potatoes, vegetables, meat and fish. Indian cooking, on the other hand, often uses a wider combination of spices, herbs and vegetables, although food habits vary considerably in both countries.

I've also noticed that some Indian vegetables are more expensive or harder to find in Irish supermarkets. For example, I might not always find fresh okra, curry leaves or the particular varieties of brinjal that I'm used to seeing in India. Some of these ingredients are imported, which can affect their availability and price.

In India, many of these ingredients are widely available in local markets, so they can be relatively affordable, depending on the region and the season.

Of course, Ireland grows much more than just potatoes and carrots. Farmers also grow cereals, vegetables and other crops. Supermarkets import many products from other countries as well.

Overall, I think the differences come down to climate, farming conditions, local demand, food traditions and the availability of ingredients.

Personally, I find it interesting to see how food habits change from one country to another. Even though the ingredients may be different, food is an important part of everyday life in both cultures.

## 4. Practise a Conversation with an Irish Colleague

**Colleague:** Have you noticed any differences between Irish and Indian food?

**You:** Yes, definitely. I've noticed that some vegetables are easier to find here than others.

**Colleague:** Really? What do you mean?

**You:** Well, potatoes, carrots and onions are available in most supermarkets. But sometimes I find it difficult to get fresh okra, curry leaves and certain Indian vegetables.

**Colleague:** Oh, I see. Why do you think that is?

**You:** I think it's partly because of the climate and partly because of local demand. Some vegetables grow better in warmer countries.

**Colleague:** That makes sense. Do you cook Indian food every day?

**You:** Yes, quite often. We use different spices and herbs to give our food more flavour.

**Colleague:** That sounds nice. What's your favourite spice?

**You:** I use cumin, coriander, turmeric, ginger and garlic quite regularly. They add a lot of flavour to our dishes.

### Practice Tip

Read both parts aloud. Then try answering without looking at the example.

## 5. Three Grammar Patterns to Remember

### Pattern 1: "I've noticed that..."

Use this when describing something you have observed.

Examples:

- I've noticed that vegetables are sometimes more expensive here.
- I've noticed that my colleagues have different food habits.
- I've noticed that the weather changes quite quickly in Ireland.

### Pattern 2: "It's harder to..."

Use this when explaining a difficulty.

Examples:

- It's harder to find certain Indian vegetables here.
- It's harder to understand some accents when people speak quickly.
- It's harder to find time to practise English when I'm busy.

### Pattern 3: "Whereas..."

Use this to compare two things.

Examples:

- Ireland has a cooler climate, whereas many parts of India are warmer.
- In Ireland, I often buy vegetables from supermarkets, whereas in India, I usually buy them from local markets.
- Some ingredients are easy to find here, whereas others are more readily available in India.

## 7. Your Final Speaking Challenge

**Question:**

"What is the biggest difference you've noticed between food habits in Ireland and India, and why do you think that difference exists?"

Try to speak for 30–45 seconds.

First, give your main observation. Then explain one or two reasons and finish with your opinion.

### Optional Speaking Structure

- I've noticed that...
- One reason might be...
- Another reason could be...
- Personally, I think...

### A Short Version to Practise from Memory

I've noticed that food habits are quite different in Ireland and India. In Ireland, I often see potatoes, carrots and onions in supermarkets. In India, we use a wider variety of vegetables, herbs and spices.

I think the differences are partly due to the climate, local demand and food traditions. Some Indian ingredients are harder to find here, and they can be more expensive.

It's interesting to experience different cuisines and learn about food in both countries.

# Part 2: Talking About Your Daily Routine

## 8. Reading Passage: My Daily Routine

I usually wake up around 7 a.m. and have a light breakfast. I usually have two bananas and a cup of milk.

If I have time, I go to the gym for about 30 minutes to an hour. If I have morning meetings, I sometimes skip the gym and start work instead.

I work until lunchtime, then have lunch and say my prayers. After that, I get back to work and attend meetings.

In the evening, I finish work, have a cup of tea, and spend some time talking to my wife and son.

Later, I might go to the gym, have dinner, and chat with my flatmates. Then I relax for a while before going to bed.
